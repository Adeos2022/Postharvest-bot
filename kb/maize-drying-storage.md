# Maize drying before storage

Maize should be dried to about 13% moisture before storage. Grain stored wetter than this can grow mould, and some moulds produce aflatoxin, which is harmful to people and animals.

Dry maize on clean tarpaulins or raised drying racks, not directly on bare soil. Bare ground adds dirt and stones and lets the grain take up moisture from the soil. Turn the grain regularly so it dries evenly, and cover it when rain threatens or at night.

A simple field check is to shell a few kernels and press them. Dry grain is hard and makes a sharp sound when bitten or rattled, but this is only a rough guide. A moisture meter is more reliable when one is available.

# Maize storage

Store only clean, fully dry, sound grain. Remove broken, mouldy, insect-damaged and discoloured kernels before storing.

Use clean, dry containers or stores. Hermetic (airtight) storage bags are a good way to protect grain from insects without chemicals. Raise bags off the floor on pallets and keep them away from walls to prevent dampness.

Check stored grain regularly for heat, musty smell, mould or insect holes. If grain is mouldy or smells bad, do not eat it or feed it to animals.
